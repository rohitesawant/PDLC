06 — Comparative Benefits

SDLC vs PDLC — AMS Transformation

Agentic AI acceleration on today's SDLC compared with an AI-led Build approach for an Application Maintenance, Support & Change organisation.

⚡ Bolt-On · SDLC
Agentic AI Drivers in AMS
AI agents accelerate the existing SDLC & support model
🏗️ Built-In · PDLC
AI-Led Build & Maintenance
AI models own build, change & maintenance end-to-end
01 Core Approach
Augment humans with agents
Copilot-style AI agents plug into the current AMS process — devs, testers and L1/L2/L3 engineers stay in the loop, agents speed them up.
Replace the Developer persona
An AI engine owns code build, change and maintenance. Humans move to intent, controls & validation — the AI does the heavy lifting.
02 AMS Mechanism
Ticket & code acceleration
  • AI triage of L1/L2/L3 tickets from queue
  • Quick-fix agents patch known defect patterns
  • Code & test generation assistants in IDE
  • Ontology-based knowledge & RCA lookup
Autonomous build & self-heal
  • AI builds from intent & acceptance criteria
  • Self-healing runtime — incidents prevented upstream
  • Continuous code refactor via neural engine
  • Tickets collapse into proactive change events
03 AMS Impact
Faster, but same shape
Same team structure, same SDLC stages — agents compress cycle time and reduce toil but the operating model stays recognisable.
Re-shapes the org
Support pyramids flatten, change volume drops, backlogs clear. AMS moves from reactive fire-fighting to proactive governance.
04 Productivity Uplift
20–30% ceiling
~25% steady state

60–70% and compounding

05 Knowledge & Learning
Human-curated knowledge
Vector & graph DBs store runbooks and RCAs for agents to retrieve — knowledge quality is capped by how well humans capture it.
Compounding neural memory
Each build, incident and fix strengthens the neural engine. AMS knowledge becomes a living, self-improving asset.
06 Constraints & Trade-offs
⚠ Short-term Win
  • Human still required in the loop for every change
  • Agents remain reactive — they don't redesign the system
  • Productivity plateau after the initial uplift
  • Legacy & complex codebases stay hard to crack
  • No structural shift in AMS operating model
✓ Long-term Differentiator
  • Longer runway & upfront investment required
  • Needs strong governance, controls & guardrails
  • Unlocks continuous self-healing & refactor
  • Decouples delivery speed from headcount
  • Creates a strategic AMS moat for the enterprise
AMS Outlook Where it wins
⚡
Fast to deploy, low to change Best for quick wins on existing AMS contracts & productivity SLAs.
🏗️
Re-architects AMS for the long run Best for enterprises betting on an AI-native maintenance & change organisation.
07 — Risk Assessment

Risks Associated — Both Approaches

Side-by-side risk view across five dimensions for an Application Maintenance, Support & Change organisation adopting either Agentic AI acceleration or AI-Led Build.

⚡ Bolt-On · SDLC
Agentic AI Risks
Accelerating today's AMS model with AI agents
🏗️ Built-In · PDLC
AI-Led Build Risks
Replacing humans with AI for code build & maintenance
01 Delivery & Quality
Hallucinated fixes & missed edge cases Medium
Agents can generate plausible-but-wrong patches on legacy or bespoke code — regressions leak to production when human review is rushed. MitigateStrict gated reviews, automated regression suites and per-agent confidence thresholds.
Model drift & emergent regressions Medium
Autonomous AI behaviour shifts as the engine learns; each release is harder to validate against prior baselines. MitigateContinuous eval harnesses, canary releases and versioned model snapshots.
02 People & Skills
Skill atrophy & junior pipeline dries up Medium
Engineers lose core troubleshooting muscle by leaning on agents — the pipeline for senior L3 talent weakens over time. MitigateStructured shadowing, rotation programs and agent-free deep-dive clinics.
Workforce transition shock High
Traditional developer roles shrink; organisation needs intent, prompt & AI engineers — change-management and morale risk is real. MitigateReskilling academy, persona redesign and phased role migration.
03 Governance & Compliance
Audit gap & data leakage High
Code, tickets and logs flow to third-party LLMs — IP & PII exposure, and it's hard to trace "agent vs human" on every change. MitigatePrivate model gateways, redaction, and immutable agent-action logs.
Accountability & regulatory uncertainty High
Who owns an incident when AI built the code? Emerging regulation (EU AI Act, sectoral rules) is still shifting underneath. MitigateClear AI ownership RACI, responsible-AI board and regulatory watch.
04 Cost & Commercial
Vendor lock-in & licensing creep Medium
Per-seat Copilot-style licensing compounds as adoption grows; limited negotiation leverage once workflows are embedded. MitigateAbstraction layer over providers and annual usage reviews.
Upfront investment & opex volatility High
Platform, data and compute costs are front-loaded; token & inference costs are hard to forecast at enterprise scale. MitigateFinOps guardrails, per-workload budgets and caching strategies.
05 Strategic & Transformation
Productivity plateau — no structural shift High
Gains cap at ~25–30%; underlying AMS operating model is unchanged, leaving competitors free to leapfrog with PDLC. MitigateTreat as a stepping-stone; keep PDLC roadmap live in parallel.
Early-adopter risk & unproven patterns Medium
Blueprints are still emerging — few peer references, and missteps tend to be public and costly. MitigatePhased rollout, peer benchmarks and lighthouse-application pilots.
Overall Risk Net Profile
⚡
Lower execution risk · higher strategic risk Easy to start, familiar operating model — but ceiling & plateau cap the long-term upside for AMS.
🏗️
Higher execution risk · lower strategic risk Harder to stand up, but once in place it compounds — governance, talent & FinOps controls are the critical success factors.
